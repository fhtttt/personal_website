Around 1100 BCE[^date] the Dorians came south from Doris, their old home,[^doris] crossed the gulf from Naupactus[^naupactus] and entered the Peloponnese.[^dorians] The Achaeans, who had been living around Laconia, lost their homeland. They took the north coast of the Peloponnese, the later Achaea, and drove out the Ionians living there.[^achaeans] Those Ionians had first come to that coast from Athens.[^from-athens] Now, as a whole people, they were forced back to Athens, and from there many of them left for Ionia with the Codridae, the house of Codrus, as their leading family.[^codridae] They founded Miletus, or rather took it over.[^miletus] It was there that the earliest philosophy would later be born.

That the Ionians came from Athens can also be seen in how close Attic Greek, the dialect spoken around Athens, is to Ionian Greek.[^dialect]

Today the mainland of Ionia is the western coast of Turkey, and the ruins of Miletus stand near the village of Balat.

[^date]: Thucydides gives no year, only a count from Troy: the Dorians came eighty years after its capture (see the next note). The year is the reckoning of Eratosthenes, kept by [Clement of Alexandria, *Stromata* 1.21](https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/02101.htm#:~:text=Eratosthenes%20thus%20sets%20down%20the%20dates) (trans. Wilson): "From the capture of Troy to the descent of the Heraclidæ, eighty years. From this to the founding of Ionia, sixty years; and the period following to the protectorate of Lycurgus, a hundred and fifty-nine years; and to the first year of the first Olympiad, a hundred and eight years." Counted back from the first Olympiad in 776 BCE, that puts the capture of Troy at about 1184 BCE, the Dorians at about 1104 and the founding of Ionia at about 1044. This event runs between the last two.

[^doris]: [Herodotus 8.31](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/The_History_of_Herodotus_%28Macaulay%29/Book_VIII#31) (trans. Macaulay): "they entered Doris ... lying between Malis and Phokis, the region which was in ancient time called Dryopis; this land is the mother-country of the Dorians in Peloponnese". He gives the wandering before that at [1.56](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/The_History_of_Herodotus_%28Macaulay%29/Book_I#56): "it dwelt in Pindos and was called Makednian; and thence it moved afterwards to Dryopis, and from Dryopis it came finally to Peloponnesus, and began to be called Dorian". Doris lies in central Greece, north of the Gulf of Corinth.

[^naupactus]: [Pausanias 10.38.10](https://www.theoi.com/Text/Pausanias10C.html#20) (trans. W. H. S. Jones): "I have heard it said that the Dorians under the sons of Aristomachus built here the vessels in which they crossed to the Peloponnesus, thus, it is said, giving to the place its name". The name means shipbuilding.

[^dorians]: [Thucydides 1.12.3-4](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/History_of_the_Peloponnesian_War/Book_1#1:12) (trans. Crawley): "Sixty years after the capture of Ilium, the modern Boeotians were driven out of Arne by the Thessalians ... Twenty years later, the Dorians and the Heraclids became masters of Peloponnese; so that much had to be done and many years had to elapse before Hellas could attain to a durable tranquillity undisturbed by removals, and could begin to send out colonies, as Athens did to Ionia and most of the islands". Ilium is Troy. The Boeotians' move is a separate event and has nothing to do with the Ionians.

[^achaeans]: [Strabo 8.7.1](https://penelope.uchicago.edu/Thayer/E/Roman/Texts/Strabo/8G%2A.html#7.1) (trans. H. L. Jones): "Now the Achaeans were Phthiotae in race, but they lived in Lacedaemon; and when the Heracleidae prevailed, the Achaeans ... attacked the Ionians, and proving themselves more powerful than the Ionians drove them out and took possession of the land themselves ... and named the country Achaea." Lacedaemon is Laconia, and the Heracleidae are the leaders of the Dorians.

[^from-athens]: [Strabo 8.7.1](https://penelope.uchicago.edu/Thayer/E/Roman/Texts/Strabo/8G%2A.html#7.1) (trans. H. L. Jones): "the Athenians even sent forth a colony of Ionians to the Peloponnesus, and caused the country which they occupied to be called Ionia after themselves instead of Aegialus".

[^codridae]: [Strabo 8.7.1](https://penelope.uchicago.edu/Thayer/E/Roman/Texts/Strabo/8G%2A.html#7.1) (trans. H. L. Jones): "But after the return of the Heracleidae they were driven out by the Achaeans and went back again to Athens; and from there they sent forth with the Codridae the Ionian colony to Asia, and these founded twelve cities on the seaboard of Caria and Lydia".

[^miletus]: [Herodotus 1.146](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/The_History_of_Herodotus_%28Macaulay%29/Book_I#146) (trans. Macaulay): "those of them who set forth to their settlements from the City Hall of Athens and who esteem themselves the most noble by descent of the Ionians, these, I say, brought no women with them to their settlement, but took Carian women, whose parents they slew ... This happened at Miletos." So Carians were already living there. And [Herodotus 9.97](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/The_History_of_Herodotus_%28Macaulay%29/Book_IX#97): "a temple of the Eleusinian Demeter, which Philistos the son of Pasicles erected when he had accompanied Neileus the son of Codros for the founding of Miletos".

[^dialect]: [Strabo 8.1.2](https://penelope.uchicago.edu/Thayer/E/Roman/Texts/Strabo/8A%2A.html#1.2) (trans. H. L. Jones): "we may say that the Ionic is the same as the ancient Attic, for the Attic people of ancient times were called Ionians, and from that stock sprang those Ionians who colonised Asia and used what is now called the Ionic speech".
